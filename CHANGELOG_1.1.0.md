# Xiaoxiang Realm Expansion — 1.1.0

Everything since 1.0.3 is in here - the updates that would have been 1.0.4
through 1.0.9, and the big one. The six sections at the top always say
where things stand: what is new, what changed, what was fixed, what is in but
not finished, what you may run into, and what was caught before you could.
Below them, the whole story of the mod as it stands, so one page tells it all.

---

## 🆕 New

### 💎 The Realm Stone, and the keystone that opens the Myriad Gates (work in progress)

Every realm now has a core, and in every core a single glowing crystal. At
the heart of each plane of the Myriad Realms, high in the middle band, stands
a chamber of Spirit Obsidian with a doorway in each wall, held by the
strongest wild cultivators there are; deep beneath the centre of the
Overworld, the Nether, the End and every far world lies a sealed one, twenty
blocks above the world's floor. Only a netherite pickaxe breaks the crystal
out whole as a Realm Stone - any other way it is lost - and whoever ascends
is handed one by the heavens as they arrive.

A stone is the start of a long road. Cut it with a netherite pickaxe at a
crafting table for ten Realm Shards (the pickaxe is spent); let the shards
lie in High-grade spiritual water or better until they turn to Realm Essence
- two minutes in High, eighty seconds in Top, forty in Supreme, twenty in
Immortal; then set essence round Spirit Obsidian between Supreme spirit stone
blocks for a Realm Keystone.

The keystone is what the Myriad Gates want. A Myriad Gate raises its twin above
only once a keystone has been set into it, and spends it on the raising.
From above, a Heaven Gate crafted with a keystone can be set down anywhere in
the Myriad Realms: it hands over its sigil, and that sigil used on a
Myriad Gate below binds the two into one way up and down. Still to come: the stone's many
other uses, and the greed it wakes in every cultivator who senses it.

### 🏮 Towns, houses and landmarks of the East

The Myriad Realms are lived in now. Walled towns stand on the middle islands
- a stone gateway in the south wall, a main street climbing to the temple, a
cross street with a roofed well where the two meet, a market of stalls on
either side, a teahouse, an inn with its cellar in the stone of its base, and
the townsfolk's houses behind - in two sizes, the greater with courtyard
houses of four wings round their courts. Hamlets of three to six houses
gather round a well of their own, and lone houses stand by themselves: a
farmer's cottage beside his fields of herbs, a merchant's house of two storeys
with the shop below, a cultivator's retreat high on its stilts, a hermit's
hut beside his lily pond, a courtyard house behind its wall.

Between them rise the landmarks: pagodas of five, seven and nine storeys with
a ring of formation runes round their feet, pavilions on islands in lotus
ponds, arched stone gateways across the roads, arched bridges and covered
bridges over streams, a walled garden entered through its round moon
doorway, watchtowers, and a bell and drum tower. Every one is laid in its
plane's own style, with cushions, boxes of herbs, small alchemy corners,
lamps of spirit stone, spirit fields and spiritual water. Wild cultivators
live in all of them, and their chests hold household stores, the market's
spirit stones, pills and herbs, the teahouse's tea things, the inn's cellar
and a pagoda's relics. Ember's burning ground has Infernal towns, hamlets and
landmarks of its own. They keep to the middle and high islands, and like the
palaces bring a foundation of rock of their own.

### 🏯 Palaces and sects of the East

Ancient palaces now stand across the Myriad Realms: a grand walled compound
with its towered entrance, side halls and a nine-bay throne hall on three
terraces; an audience hall on its raised platform; a sky palace on a cloud;
a water palace on piles over a lotus pool; a mountain palace climbing in
terraces to its summit hall. Sect compounds stand among them - a sect's
great arch at the head of its long stair, a sect hall over its training
ground, the disciples' rows of rooms, an elder's peak. Each rises in the style of
the plane it stands in: green-glazed roofs, blood peach pillars and jade in
the Verdant Plane; blackstone, red lacquer and gold in the Infernal;
purpur, end stone and violet glass in the Ender. Down in the Forsaken Depths
lie the overgrown ruins of immortals' palaces fallen from the planes above,
each with a sealed vault beneath it. The Jade Giant holds Verdant palaces
of its own, and Ember Infernal ones.

They are cultivators' places through and through. A spirit vein core lies
under every throne, feeding a formation core plate through its runes; there
are meditation cushions, alchemy halls round their cores, spirit fields of
herbs, pools of spiritual water, sect flags at the entrances and spirit stone in
the floors. Wild cultivators live in them - disciples and elders in the
sects, guards and a master in the palaces - and the higher the islands, the
higher their realms. The chests hold what such places would: palace
treasuries, stores and hall shrines, sect storerooms, disciples' chests and
elders' treasuries, with spirit stones, pills, refining scrolls, seeds, the
plane's own swords and goods - and an immortal's hoard in a ruin's vault -
richer the grander the place.

A palace stands about every two thousand blocks, a sect compound a little
more often. Every one rests on a foundation of its own rock, so where its
island ends it reaches out over the open sky like an island of its own; and
none ever stands on another, or on a sky shrine, a fire altar or a jade
mine.

### 🗺️ The Myriad Realms grow vast, and tell you where you stand

The Myriad Realms are now laid out in rings round their centre, beneath the
Overworld's spawn. The Verdant Plane runs sixty thousand blocks from the
centre every way. Round it lie two rings of planes, each a hundred and
twenty thousand blocks deep: five planes in the first ring and six in the
second. Past three hundred thousand blocks lies the Immortal Plane, and it
runs all the way to the edge of the world; the Immortal Sea will be one of
its lands. Every plane is at least fifty thousand blocks across, its
borders bend and wander, and its lands are about three thousand blocks
across. Each world's seed decides the order of the planes in each ring and
how far each ring is turned, so no two worlds lie alike. Even on the
fastest sword of the highest realms, crossing a plane is a journey, never a
step.

A plane whose lands have not yet arrived still keeps its place: until they
come, the planes on either side share its ground, half each, and a ring
with no lands of its own yet is held by the ring inside it. So no plane
ever turns up in two places, and every border you cross is a real one. For
now the Infernal and Ender Planes share everything outside the Verdant
Plane.

A second after you arrive in the Myriad Realms - through a Myriad Gate, by
the ascent or any other way - a line tells you which plane you stand in,
how far away its nearest border lies and in which direction; opening the
Star Chart there tells you again. Arrivals still set you down over your own
ground, so from near the Overworld's spawn you arrive deep in the Verdant
Plane. Records of the Myriad Realms has a new page, The Lie of the Planes,
with every size.

### 🏝️ The planes take their own shapes, and the Forsaken Depths lie beneath them all

The lands of the Myriad Realms no longer all float alike. The Lava Seas
spread out in wide, low shelves; the End Barrens break into high, ragged
shelves; the Starlit Expanse drifts as thin, flat isles at three heights;
the Void Shards scatter many small islands from low to high; the Misty
Mountains rise as tall islands; and the Terrace Islands climb in stepped
tiers. Where two lands of different shapes meet, one gives way to the other
a little at a time over a few dozen blocks, so an island that runs over the
border bends into its neighbour instead of ending in a wall. The Spirit
Forests' islands keep exactly the shape they had, to the last block.

The Verdant Plane now has all six of its lands. Around the Spirit Forests lie
the Bamboo Highlands, thick with bamboo groves, where pandas and parrots live
and spore blossoms drift; the Flower Meadows, with cherry trees and patches
of spiritual flowers under falling petals; the Lotus Lakes, jade-green lakes
among spiritual lily pads, swamp oaks and sugar cane, with frogs and glowing
motes; the Misty Mountains, tall islands of pines and mossy boulders where
goats climb and white mist hangs in the air; and the Terrace Islands, whose
stepped tops hold square fields of wheat rimmed with azalea. Every one of
them is crowned with the band's spiritual grass, the higher grass on the
higher islands.

Beneath every plane lie the Forsaken Depths, the lowest band of islands, the
same whichever plane is above: grey dead stone, withered trees with no
leaves, cobblestone rubble and broken grey ruins holding a chest and a soul
lantern, all under a dark fog and a cold blue-grey sky, with ash on the air
and the deep dark's music. Monsters are at their thickest down there.

### 🌌 Every plane of the Myriad Realms under its own sky

Cross into a plane of the Myriad Realms and the sky changes with it, one sky
blending into the next over sixty blocks. The Verdant Plane keeps the clear
sky it has always had, with its sun, its moon, its clouds and the Spirit
Moon. The Infernal Plane lies under a red haze, thickest at the horizon, with
no clouds. The Ender Plane lies under the End's own dark sky, with no sun and
no moon. The planes still to come have their skies waiting for the day their
lands appear: an aurora over the Frost Plane, two moons over the Lunar Plane,
stars by day over the Star Plane, a golden sky over the Immortal Sea and a
storm-dark sky over the Thunder Plane.

Where two planes meet, a thin veil of light stands along the border, coloured
by both planes and rippling slowly. You see it from afar, and it fades away
as you come near, so it is never a wall in front of you. Cross it and the
plane's name comes up on your screen with the name of the land under it,
once a crossing; walking along a border shows nothing.

Each plane has its own air and weather too: pollen drifts in the Verdant
Plane, ash in the Infernal Plane and motes of the void in the Ender Plane.
When it rains, ash falls on the Infernal Plane and stars fall on the Ender
Plane, and the planes to come have their snowstorm, golden rain and thunder.
Each plane has its own song as well - the game's Nether and End music for the
Infernal and Ender Planes - and as you cross, one song fades out and the next
fades in. The veil and the names each have their own switch, and one setting
shows any plane's sky anywhere in the realm so you can look at all of them.

### 🌀 Two ways into the Myriad Realms

The sky no longer opens into the islands. Rise past the Overworld's sky and
you go where the realm's own top goes - into the space mod's solar system,
into Earth's orbit with Ad Astra, into the Xiao System with neither - and
where there is no space at all, the sky simply ends, as the game's own does.
A server that wants the old sky door back can switch it on.

There are now two ways into the Myriad Realms: the last tribulation, which
carries an Earth Immortal up, and the **Myriad Gate**. It is a formation five
blocks by five - the Myriad Gate block in the middle, Spirit Obsidian round
it, spirit stone blocks on the edges and High-grade blocks at the corners -
and the block itself costs four High spirit stones, four Supreme spirit stone
blocks and Spirit Obsidian. Feed it spirit stones and a ring of light turns
over it; step into the ring and you rise into the islands over your own
ground, mount and passengers with you. Nothing that hunts you follows.

The first crossing raises its twin above, on the nearest island or on a
Spirit Obsidian platform made for it; the twin's ring takes you back down,
free.
An ascended immortal cannot go down through a Myriad Gate - their way down is
still their own Ascension Gate. Operators can still teleport anyone, and can
send a player up into the realm or back to where they came from.

### ⚡ Nine Heaven realms above Earth Immortal

The road that began at MORTAL no longer ends at Earth Immortal. The moment
the heavens carry an Earth Immortal up into the Myriad Realms (at once, where
a server has switched Ascension off), the climb begins again: Heaven
Immortal, Mystic Immortal, Golden Immortal, Taiyi Golden Immortal, Daluo
Golden Immortal, Immortal Venerable, Immortal King, Immortal Emperor and Dao
Ancestor - nine realms of ten layers each, every realm asking ten times the
one before. Qi fills the bar as it always has; a full bar and the
breakthrough key take you up a layer, and every layer pays what the layers
of Tribulation Transcendence pay - attributes that keep climbing, Zhenyuan,
and lifespan where your settings grant it. On the steepest curves, where the
numbers would run out, the realms still to come share what is left, so every
layer still asks at least a twentieth more than the last. A Loose Immortal,
who chose to stay in the world, never climbs.

At the top of every realm the heavens answer with a tribulation - ten waves
of ten bolts the first time, ten more waves and harder bolts in every realm
after - and its bolts are true tribulation lightning: standing in spiritual
water softens them as it softens every tribulation, a wicked heart draws
harder strikes, a sect's protection dome takes its share, and no technique
turns them aside. Survive and you cross into the next realm. Die under the
storm or leave the world while it rages and you fall back a layer, and the
heavens stay closed for five minutes once your bar is full again; if the
server itself stops mid-storm, the storm is simply called off and you come
back at the tenth layer as you were.

Wherever you stand shows: "Golden Immortal, Layer 7" on the HUD, on the
cultivation screen and over your head for everyone who sees you. Operators
can raise anyone straight into a Heaven realm with the same command that
raises through the realms below - the heavens carry them up first, and every
layer on the way pays what the climb pays. The Heaven Realms page of the
settings sets the waves, the bolts, their strength and the wait.

### 🌌 The Myriad Realms become a world of many planes

The Myriad Realms are no longer one sky of islands. The realm is now laid
out in great swaths, each a plane of its own - now vast, and laid out in
rings (above) - all in the same world: the Verdant Plane, where the floating islands
you know still drift in their three bands with their spirit grass, found
pools and still falls; the Infernal Plane, the Nether's echo; and the Ender
Plane, the End's. Cross a border and the sky, the fog, the motes in the air
and the creatures change around you - you never leave the realm.

The Infernal Plane holds six lands under a red haze: Crimson Groves of huge
fungi and weeping vines, Warped Thickets, Basalt Reaches of columns and
deltas, Soul Valleys of soul sand, bone and blue fire, Lava Seas whose lakes
are fed by falls of lava that never spill into the clouds, and Blackstone
Bastions, where the broken walls of small keeps still hold a chest of the
plane's goods. Fire on its netherrack burns forever, and its piglins and
hoglins do not turn. The Ender Plane holds five lands under a dark sky and
purple fog: Chorus Highlands, End Barrens, Purpur Spires (ruined towers
still kept by shulkers, with a chest at their foot), Void Shards of obsidian
and crystal hanging in the open air, and the Starlit Expanse under a
night-blue sky.

In a world you already have, everything you have explored stays exactly as
it was. The new planes appear only where nobody has been yet, so at the
edge of explored ground an island may change its crown along a straight
line.

### ☁️ Ascension: the heavens call

Survive the last tribulation and the heavens open over you: a pillar of light
seen from afar, a chord rising each second, and a ten-second count - "The
heavens call you". Then you are carried up into the Myriad Realms and set
down on the highest island above where you stood, or on a platform of Spirit
Obsidian made for you. "Ascended." From then on, should you die, you wake in
the Myriad Realms - where you were set down, or at a bed of yours up there. A
Loose Immortal chose to stay, and stays.

The mortal worlds close behind you. The Overworld, the Nether and the End -
and any world your server adds - no longer take you, by portal, door or
teleporter: "The mortal world can no longer hold you." Fall off the bottom of
the Sky Realm and the heavens catch you.

Only an Ascension Gate raised before you rise leads back down. Make it from
Supreme spirit stone blocks, Spirit Obsidian and an Immortal spirit stone,
set it in a mortal world, and when you ascend its Gate Stone rises beside
you. Stand on the stone and use it: the mortal world holds you for ten
minutes, a bar counting down, while the ground trembles under you, glass, ice
and leaves crack and break, and animals run - never in claimed land or by
spawn. Then the heavens draw you back up. Six settings on the Ascension page
of the Xiao Universe tab.

### ⚡ Raise a cultivator to any realm in one command

Operators can raise players straight to a realm and layer with
`/realmexpansion raise <players> <realm> <layer>`, or to the very top - the
last layer of Tribulation Transcendence - with `/realmexpansion raise
<players> top`. Every layer on the way is broken through by the cultivation
mod's own breakthrough, so a raised player ends up exactly like one who
climbed: every attribute point, Zhenyuan and year of life the layers and
realms pay under the curve in force, and full qi for the new realm. The
player and the operator are both told what the raise paid. Raising never
lowers anyone: a target below where a player stands is refused before
anything changes. Every raise and every refusal is written to the server log.

### 💎 Spirit stones in nine levels

Every spirit stone now comes in nine levels, and a stone of level n holds
exactly n times its level one: a Low-grade stone 10 qi at level one and 90 at
level nine, a Mid-grade 100 to 900, a High-grade 1,000 to 9,000, a Supreme
10,000 to 90,000 - and above them the new **Immortal Spirit Stone**, 100,000 to
900,000 Immortal Spiritual Energy, a thousand qi each. Every figure ends in a
zero, so stones divide and add up cleanly, and no grade's ninth level ever
reaches the next grade's first. A stone shows its level as nine slices lit
clockwise from the top, and the whole ladder has a tab of its own.

**Steep them.** Crouch and drop a stone into spiritual water of its grade or
better and it steeps, glimmering, climbing a level every five minutes; the
ninth level asks for water one grade richer, and Immortal water carries an
Immortal stone all the way. Thrown in standing up, a stone below Immortal pays
a pond as before, at its full worth. From the sixth level on, a stone you take
in feeds your cultivation as well as your qi.

**Wear them.** With Curios installed, a Supreme or Immortal stone worn in the
charm slot feeds your cultivation every second, and the same reaches every
cultivator and every cultivating tamed beast within eight blocks - and the
cultivators you help warm to you as the minutes pass. A small stone in the
worn stone's colour sits at the end of your cultivation bar while it works.
Air Arrays, ponds, cultivators' trades and their greed weigh a stone of any
level at its worth, and a ninth-level stone presses into a spirit stone block
at the Refining Furnace just as nine stones do. Every number is on the new
**Spirit Stones** page.

### 💎 A treasure for every world

Eight of the worlds past the Overworld now hold a treasure found nowhere
else. **Cloud Stone** lies in the rock of the Sky Realm's middle and high
islands, **Moon Dust** glimmers in the Spirit Moon's jade, **Jade Shards**
wait in the Jade Giant's islands, **Ember Cinder** smoulders in Ember's
rock, **Frost Crystal** hides in Frostvein's stone, **Star Iron** runs
through the Asteroid Belt, **Comet Ice** is packed into the Comet, and
**Immortal Pearls** sit in pearl beds on the floor of the Immortal Realm's
sea. Each wants a pickaxe, and the wild cultivators born on those worlds
carry a few of their home's treasure and sell them for spirit stones.

Every one of them makes something. Cloud Stone becomes the **Hanging
Cloud**, which stays wherever you set it, even in thin air, and the **Cloud
Cushion**, which takes the hurt out of any fall. Moon Dust lights the
**Moon Lamp** and **Lunar Glass**, and Jade Shards are laid into **Jade
Bricks** and **Polished Jade**. Ember Cinder burns three times as long as
coal and turns sand into glass and cobblestone into stone with no furnace
at all; Frost Crystal cools a bucket of lava into obsidian and packed ice
into blue ice. Star Iron makes **tools and armour a step above iron**, and
Comet Ice makes **Everfrost Ice**, which never melts, and the **Comet
Lamp**, a cold light that leaves the ice and snow beside it alone. Immortal
Pearls press into a block that gives off pure qi, richer than Immortal
Rock, and with Moon Dust, a Jade Shard and a High spirit stone they make
the **Sea-Heart Pill**: half a day in an Immortal pool at a single swallow,
and then none for half a day. Twenty-nine recipes, in four languages.

### 🌌 The skies of our worlds

When Cosmic Horizons is not installed, our space has a sky worth looking up
at. A faint nebula of jade, violet and amber lies behind the stars, crossed by
the pale band of a far galaxy, and it never outshines the planets hanging in
front of it. The Comet streams two tails away from the sun - a straight blue
one of gas and a broad golden one of dust that curves back along its path -
and from inside the Asteroid Belt a band of distant rocks circles the whole
sky while the sun shines only half as bright through the dust.

Each of the Xiao worlds has a sight of its own. Ember's horizon smoulders under
a red-orange haze and ash drifts down wherever the sky is open; Frostvein hangs
curtains of green and violet aurora in its northern sky at night; on the Nine
Peaks a sea of cloud lies below the summits; the Jade Giant shows its other
islands far off in the haze; and in the Immortal Realm a golden ring stands
round the sun while slow sparkles glint over the sea. One switch, **Sky
Sights**, on the Our Worlds page of the Xiao Universe settings, turns all of
them off together - and with Cosmic Horizons installed none of it is drawn, so
its own skies stay exactly as they are.

The Spirit Moon in the sky of home now waxes and wanes with the game's own
moon: full when it is full, a thin crescent when it is, lit on the side the
sun is on, its dark side only faintly glowing.

### 🫧 Air in sealed rooms (work in progress)

In the worlds without air - the spaces, the airless planets, the Spirit Moon -
a room closed in on every side now holds air of its own. Wall, floor and roof
it with full blocks, glass and panes, shut doors and trapdoors, or slabs and
stairs turned so their solid faces close the gap, and step inside: beside your
breath bar you see its oxygen, its carbon dioxide and how many blocks it holds
- or that you stand open to the void. A new room starts as empty as the void
outside, and may hold up to fifty thousand blocks of air.

The **Air Array** fills it: a formation block fed spirit stones by hand or by
hopper, each grade holding far more air than the one below. It makes oxygen
until the room is full, clears a little of the breathed-out air, and its front
swirls and glows while it works. Spiritual grass, spiritual plants and a
blessing pond breathe oxygen into a room by themselves, and with Ad Astra
installed its oxygen distributor counts while it runs. In good air you breathe
as on the Overworld and your qi shield rests.

Everything alive in a room breathes it down - sprinting four times as fast as
standing still - and every flame burns it: torches, lit furnaces, campfires,
open fire, lava. Under 15% oxygen the air is thin and you are slowed and sick;
under 8%, or over 5% carbon dioxide, it is no air at all and your shield takes
over as it does outside, and a room that had good air and lost it puts its
flames out - torches drop, fires die. Immortals whose shield never empties
breathe nothing. Every number is on the new Air page of the Xiao Universe
settings. Still to come: a breach that drains a room through its hole rather
than at once, and an airlock frame.

### 🀄 Realm Expansion in Simplified and Traditional Chinese

Realm Expansion now reads in Simplified Chinese and in Traditional Chinese
(Taiwan), the two languages the cultivation mod itself ships. Every block,
item, tab, tooltip, message, death message, advancement, subtitle, world
name and line of the Star Chart has its Chinese.

The words are the cultivation mod's own wherever it has them: its grades
(下品, 中品, 上品, 极品, 仙品), its qi, cultivation, realms,
breakthroughs, sects, pills, Refining Furnace and Sword Flight. The two
grades this mod adds sit on the same ladder - Inferior is 劣品, below 下品,
and Top is 上上品, between 上品 and 极品 - so 劣品回灵丹 and 上上品回灵丹
stand beside the cultivation mod's own pills. Anything the water steeps is
灵化 (魔化 for the demonic liquid) in front of the game's own name for it,
so every steeped block, tool, piece of armour, food, bow and lantern reads
as the game names it in your language: 下品灵化铁剑, 封印仙品灵化石砖,
上上品魔化钻石剑. Traditional Chinese uses the game's Taiwan names
throughout, and the worlds have names of their own in both scripts: 云霄界,
灵月, 小翔星系, 碧玉巨星, 余烬星, 霜脉星, 九峰星 and 仙界.

### 🎵 Every world has its own song

The worlds of the Xiao System have music now, each its own. The Sky Realm's
theme comes in three versions - quiet and pastoral among the low islands,
brighter through the middle band, sparse and airy at the very top - and the
Spirit Moon, the Jade Giant, Ember, Frostvein, the Nine Peaks, the Asteroid
Belt, the Comet, the Immortal Realm and deep space each have a piece of
their own, played on zither, bamboo flute, bells, temple gong and frame drum
in the old five-note modes, every world in its own mode, key and pace. A
song comes now and then, the way the game's own music does, and never cuts
across another.

Between songs every world sounds like itself: wind over the islands, the
Moon's low hum, the rumble and crackle of Ember, ice creaking on Frostvein,
a mountain stream on the Nine Peaks, rock grinding in the Belt, surf on the
Immortal Realm's sea and the deep drone of space - with something rarer
drifting past now and then, a crane, a far temple bell, a hawk, a golden
bell over the water. And what happens can be heard: a door between worlds
opening as something leaves and again where it arrives, war drums and a horn
when a raid lands and a cry when a raider runs, a chime as a pond takes you
into its blessing, one bell as the Spirit Moon rises at dusk, and, in the
Xiao System's own space, the moment weight falls away and the moment it
comes back.

Every one of them has a subtitle. **World Music** and **World Ambience** on
the Our Worlds page can each be turned off; turning one off mid-song stops

# What 1.0.3 brought, kept here so the whole story is in one place

Spiritual water got thicker, harder to make, and one grade taller. The new top
grade is gold.

## 🏅 Immortal Spiritual Water

A seventh grade above Supreme. **Gold as honey and glossy with it**, lit from
within at a soft light of nine — enough to light the ground for eight blocks
around a pool, which is eight blocks nothing hostile will spawn on. It is
**warm**: ice beside it melts, snow on the bank thins away, and anything that
walks in frozen thaws out within a second. To a machine it is a heat source:
it is as hot as a fluid can be short of lava, and it counts as lava to a heat
generator of the Mekanism kind standing beside it. It is not lava to anything
alive - nothing burns in it, and nothing dropped into it is lost.

It gives **sixty-four times** the qi of Inferior and reaches **ninety blocks**
as a Blessed Land. It takes a pond of at least nine blocks to make — a single
block will refuse the stones.

**Every Grade Glows**, a switch on the Spiritual Water page, lights the other
grades too: one at Inferior and a step more each grade, six at Supreme, so a
pond's grade shows at night - never brighter than Immortal Light, and off
unless you turn it on. Like Immortal Light, it takes effect on restart.

## 💎 The ladder is sixteen stones a rung

Making and raising spiritual water is one rule now, and the rule is **throw in
sixteen stones of the right grade for every source block** — a quarter of a
stack a block, and a number you can change: one block of water takes sixteen,
a nine-block pond 144, a Blessed Land's eighty-one 1,296. The stones **vanish
the moment they touch the water**. Any number at a time; **the pond keeps count
with the world**, through restarts, and a **floating count hangs over it** -
"40 / 144 Low Spirit Stones - to Mid-grade" - as well as the action bar after
each throw. Whatever the pond does not need stays in the world.

| to reach | priced at | into |
|---|---|---|
| Inferior | 16 low spirit stones a block | plain water, a block at a time |
| Low | 16 low | Inferior |
| Mid | 16 mid | Low |
| High | 16 high | Mid |
| Top | 16 supreme | High |
| Supreme | 16 supreme | Top |
| Immortal | 16 supreme | Supreme, nine blocks or more |

**Any grade of stone pays, at its worth.** A rung is priced in its own grade -
sixteen mid stones a block to climb into Mid - but every grade pays towards it
at the cultivation mod's own rates: a low stone is worth ten, a mid stone a
hundred, a high stone a thousand, a supreme stone ten thousand. Ten low stones
do what one mid stone does; one supreme stone does what a hundred mid stones
do. After each throw the action bar says how many more **of the stone you just
threw** it takes - throw a low stone and it counts in low stones, throw a high
one and it counts in high. The count over the pond stays in the rung's own
stone, to one decimal place.

The stone that finishes a rung pays only what was left, and **the rest floats
back up as change**, biggest stones first - a supreme stone dropped on a rung
that wanted 160 gives back nine high, eight mid and four low. Change still in
the water goes on towards the next rung, so one supreme stone thrown into a
puddle can carry it up several grades; at the top, or in a pond too small for
its next rung, it simply floats for you to pick up. Nothing is lost, and change
is never a better stone than the one you threw.

Each step still needs the water of the step below, so there is no skipping Low
to buy Mid from a bucket of water. Worlds that were part-way up a rung keep
what they had paid.

**The crafting-bench recipe is gone.** The bench made it a thing you craft;
it is a thing you raise.

## 💠 Spirit stone blocks: nine stones in, ten stones' worth

Every grade of spirit stone has a block now: **Low-grade, Mid-grade, High-grade
and Supreme Spirit Stone Blocks**. Nine stones of one grade press into a block
at the **Refining Furnace** - a material recipe that spends one stone's worth of
the furnace's own qi (10, 100, 1,000 or 10,000) - and a crafting table breaks a
block back into nine.

A block **counts for ten stones** wherever its qi is used:

- **Thrown into a pond**, it counts as ten stones of its grade towards the next
  rung, so two low blocks wake a block of plain water. When the pond needs
  fewer than a block holds, the last block breaks open: the pond takes what it
  needs and the rest float back up as loose stones - which, like any change
  left in the water, go on towards the next rung. Nothing is lost.
- **Sneak and hold use toward open air** and you draw on it the way a stone is
  eaten: ten stones' qi at once - 100 from a low block, 100,000 from a supreme
  one. Without the sneak it only ever places, so building with them never eats
  one.

Placed, a block wears its stone's colour and a soft glow that brightens with
the grade, and any pickaxe mines it.

The press lives at the furnace because the crafting grid's three-by-three of
one stone is already the cultivation mod's own climb - nine low stones make a
mid stone - and a second recipe on the same grid would let the game hand you
either one. How many stones a block counts for is **Stone Block Worth** on the
Spiritual Water page, ten by default; the crafting table always gives back
nine, so pressing and breaking never makes stones.

**Slabs and stairs** of every grade's block: on the stonecutter (two slabs or
one stair a block) and on the crafting grid in the game's own patterns, each in
its block's colour and glow and mined as it is. They are for building and
hold no qi - thrown into a pond they count for nothing, they cannot be drawn
on, and nothing turns them back into blocks or stones - so cutting a block is
its qi spent on looks, and never makes stones.

## 🌊 Every grade falls, and travels as far as its grade allows

Spiritual water of any grade pours down a hole beneath it and fills what is
below - thick as it is, it is still a liquid. Sideways it travels on a budget
set by its grade: **Inferior thirteen blocks, Low eleven, Mid nine, High
seven, Top five, Supreme three, Immortal one.** **It flows as plain water
does**: the block you pour is a source and the rest is a spill that drains when
the source goes. It makes no source of its own - not where it comes to rest,
and not between two sources the way plain water does - so **a pond grows only
beside Spirit Obsidian**, the fuel it feeds on, and climbs with the spirit
stones thrown in. Ponds made before this update stay as they are. The reach,
the step per grade and a switch that lets resting water become a source again
are on the Spiritual Water page; Config Extension's **Infinite Sources** page
can turn the two-sources rule back on for a grade. Wading is slower the better the
water.

## 🪨 It grows beside Spirit Obsidian

Spiritual water does not multiply on its own, and it has nothing to do with
ordinary water. What it feeds on is **Spirit Obsidian**, the stone that is
made of it: a source block touching the stone grows, now and then, into the
water or the air beside it. Line a basin with Spirit Obsidian and the pool
fills itself; a pool with no stone beside it stays exactly the size it was
poured. Inferior grows at about a block every twenty minutes, Immortal every
five, and the speed is yours to set.

**Top, Supreme and Immortal source blocks cannot be overwritten.** A block
placed into one or a sand column dropped on one leaves the water where it was.
No bucket of anything pours into spiritual water of any grade. To move it,
carry it away in a bucket.

## 🧱 Spirit Obsidian, cut

Spirit Obsidian goes on the **stonecutter**: bricks, a slab, stairs, a wall
and a pillar from the stone, and a slab, stairs and a wall from the bricks.
Eight new blocks. The crafting grid makes them too, in the patterns you
already know - four stone in a square for bricks, a row of three for slabs,
the step of six for stairs, two rows of three for walls, two stacked for a
pillar.

Every cut keeps the stone's glow and its blast resistance, needs a diamond
pickaxe like the stone does, and **still quickens the water beside it** - a
pond rimmed with stairs grows as one rimmed with blocks does.

**Spirit Obsidian Glass**: four glass set around a block of Spirit Obsidian
on the crafting grid make four. The game's glass in a jade frame, as hard and
as blast-proof as the stone, faintly lit - and the stone still, so a pond
walled in it grows as one rimmed with the stone does, and you can see into it.

## 🧊 Hardened Spirit Obsidian holds the water

Smelt Spirit Obsidian - a furnace, or a blast furnace in half the time - and
it comes out **Hardened Spirit Obsidian**: darker, tougher, with its qi sealed
in. It is the stone's other half. Spirit Obsidian feeds the water; the
hardened stone holds it. Spiritual water touching it is **still** - it does not
grow, even with Spirit Obsidian beside it too, and it does not take the poorer
water next to it - and **nothing grows onto it**. Line a basin's floor with the
one and its rim with the other, and the pool fills the basin and stops at the
rim.

It needs a diamond pickaxe, takes half as long again as obsidian to mine,
stands up to explosions like obsidian, and a piston cannot move it. It gives
no light.

## 🩸 Demonic liquid - the water's dark twin

Seven grades, Inferior to Immortal, blood red at the bottom to near-black at
the top. **It eats.** A source block now and then takes a block beside it -
any side, up included - and that block becomes more of it, so a pool left
alone keeps growing: a block about every eight minutes for Inferior, about
every minute for Immortal. Only three things hold it: blocks nothing can
break, **Hardened Spirit Obsidian**, and **spiritual water of a higher grade**.
Equal or poorer spiritual water is eaten like anything else, and so are chests
and furnaces, with what is inside them, unless the setting spares them.

Whoever stands in it **burns**, is **poisoned** - withered as well from Top up -
and takes a point more damage a second for each grade. A player or a wandering
cultivator **loses morality** every second, 1 in Inferior doubling to 64 in
Immortal, so a long soak puts a cultivator on the evil path.

**Creatures turn demonic.** Anything that wades in comes out tougher, stronger,
faster and harder to knock back - half again at Inferior, three times at
Immortal - never burns again, heals in the liquid, and hunts every living thing
that has not turned. A cow is given a blow of its own; a skeleton or a creeper
keeps its own and aims it at everything. Bosses and wandering cultivators never
turn.

**Where to find it.** Walled springs form in newly made land: Inferior, Low and
Mid in the Nether; High to Immortal in the outer End, the richest the rarest.
Each is a small pool in a shell of Hardened Spirit Obsidian, open at the top -
take it with a bucket. It stays put until the shell is broken or something
comes to rest on top of it. Every number - whether it eats, how fast, whether
it spares containers, morality a second, whether creatures turn, whether
springs form - is on the new **Demonic Liquid** page.

**Demonic gear.** The liquid steeps tools and armour as the water does: leave
an ordinary piece in it for ten seconds and it comes out **demonic** at the
liquid's grade, drawn as the piece it was with the liquid's red over it -
every piece in every grade, 378 of them, on seven Demonic Gear tabs of their
own. The numbers are the spiritual gear's, grade for grade. **A demonic weapon
or tool withers what it strikes** for three seconds - Wither I up to Mid, II
to Supreme, III at Immortal - and **demonic armour keeps the liquid's harm off
you**, a quarter a piece: its fire, its blows, its poison and its wither, so a
full set walks through it. Not its corruption: the morality it takes still
goes. Each side takes the other's gear where it is of a lower grade, as each
takes the other's grass - a Mid spiritual sword left in High demonic liquid
comes out a High demonic one, and the other way round; enchantments, damage
and names go with it. The liquid leaves pills alone and cleanses nothing.

**Demonic gear is safe only on the evil path.** Hold a demonic weapon or tool
in either hand, or wear a demonic piece, while you are not yet evil and the
gear turns on you: it **withers you** - harder for a better grade - and **drags
your morality toward evil** every second, each piece its grade's share (one a
second for Inferior, doubling a grade to sixty-four for Immortal). Once you
cross below -50, where the cultivation mod counts you evil, the gear is yours
and does you no harm - "You walk the evil path now". Wandering cultivators
are held to the same rule; creative players are left alone. Both the rule and
the pull are on the Demonic Liquid page, under Demonic Gear.

membrane draws and the aura pays inside, so what you see, what you feel and
what keeps the creepers off are one shape.

**An Immortal pond's reach heals.** A zombie villager inside it is not driven
out: it is spared and cured — the shaking a golden apple starts, as long as the
game's own cure takes — and comes back a villager. And nothing living burns
inside it: a creature or a player on fire there is put out within the second.

## 🥷 Wandering cultivators want your treasures

**They cultivate on it.** A wandering cultivator inside a blessed land takes
in its qi every second - more standing in the water itself - and its
cultivation bar rises where you can see it, with a shimmer of motes.

**They covet it.** Hold a bucket or a vessel of spiritual water in either
hand where a cultivator can see you, within twenty-four blocks, and its regard
for you falls every second it watches - faster the better the water - until it
turns on you with its own spells and blades. A stranger turns in about three
seconds over Inferior water and one over Immortal; one who likes you a great
deal holds out much longer, and a sect-mate still will not strike a sect-mate
(except in a ruthless world - below).

**How greedy they are depends on your world.** A new setting, **What They
Covet** (Wild Cultivators tab, *Their Greed*), and picking a difficulty curve
sets it to match:

| greed | curves | what they covet - in your hand and on the ground |
|---|---|---|
| None | Gentle, Gentle Plus | nothing at all - no coveting, no snatching, no scramble |
| Water | Standard, Standard Plus | spiritual water |
| Riches | Brutal, Brutal Plus, **Nine Heavens (the default)** | water; a stack of spirit stones or stone blocks worth a high stone or more; pills of High grade and up, and the breakthrough pills |
| Ruthless | Marathon, Marathon Plus | anything of worth - a single low spirit stone, any pill |

The better the treasure, the faster their regard falls: a stone at its own
grade (Low, Mid, High, Supreme), a pill at its own. In a **ruthless** world a
**sect is no shelter**: a sect-mate who turns on you over what is in your hand
will strike you, and sect-mates chase a sect-mate who runs off with a
treasure. Put the treasure away and sect law holds again. The Presets window's
Wild Cultivators heading has a row for each greed, to set it without changing
the curve.

**They snatch it - and then they fight over it.** A cultivator's blow can
knock the water out of your hand (a quarter of hits), and it lands on the
ground between you. **Every cultivator who can see a treasure on the ground
breaks off what it was doing and runs for it** - whatever your world's greed
counts as one. The first to take it drops every fight and runs from whoever is
near, and once it is hurt badly enough it slips into the void the way it would
to save its life. **Every other cultivator who sees the carrier turns on it
and gives chase** - sect law permitting. Kill the carrier and what it took
falls where it died, and the scramble starts again. It runs for a minute (a
setting) or until nobody is near. Two things are left alone: stones in
spiritual water, which are feeding the pond (and its change bobs there for
you), and a pill you throw a cultivator - that is a gift, not loot. With the
scramble switched off, the blow puts the treasure straight into the striker's
pack, as before.

**They steal from your ponds.** A cultivator with no sect, in a world that
covets water, now and then looks for spiritual water within 24 blocks of it,
walks to the edge and **fills a bucket** — one source block gone from your
pond — and you hear of it if you are within 48 blocks. From then on it is a
taker like any other: it runs, every cultivator who sees it gives chase, and
killing it drops the bucket. It never steals while you stand within six
blocks of the water. Once it is safe — its run over, nobody close, two
minutes gone — it drinks what it took for a minute's worth of a pool's qi and
keeps the empty bucket. Half of them carry a bucket; one
without makes one from three iron ingots. Each waits twenty minutes between
thefts. All three are settings on the cultivators' Their Greed page.

**They trade with each other — and some cheat.** A wild cultivator that lacks
something another near it has to spare — a cultivation pill or fruit while its
bar is not full, or a material for its own next breakthrough — buys it at the
seller's own price, the one it would ask of you, in spirit stones. They walk
up to each other and **throw**: a coin decides who goes first, the stones or
the goods, and once the first throw is caught the other throws back. A seller
lets go only of what it does not need itself. And sometimes the one who caught
the first throw **keeps it and runs**: the one it cheated turns on it, every
cultivator who sees it gives chase, and killing it drops what it took — one in
ten by default, twice as often in a Ruthless world, twice again for a
cultivator on the evil path, never where nothing is coveted. A fight or a
trade with you calls the deal off, and whatever was thrown lies on the ground,
anybody's. Three settings on the same page.

**Death gives it back to the world.** A player who dies always drops their
spiritual water where they fell - buckets and vessels holding it, from the
whole inventory - even when the world keeps inventories on death. A death that
something prevents keeps it in hand. Each of these is a switch on the
Spiritual Water page.

## 🧘 Wild cultivators climb in layers

**Ten layers a realm, like yours.** Wild cultivators now climb in layers from
Mortal to Tribulation Transcendence: three each to Early, Middle and Late, and
one to Peak. Every layer asks its own amount, so a Mortal Early cultivator no
longer needs what a Mortal Peak one does.

**You can see it.** The name over a cultivator's head says its layer - *Mortal
Late Layer 7* - and so does its Details page. The cultivation figure there is
the real requirement, written out in full up to 99,999 and short above that:
two million reads 2M.

**They move up on their own.** A full bar inside a stage carries a cultivator
to its next layer at once, with a burst of light and a chime. Every layer it
clears drops random attribute points into its five attributes - the same roll
your own Mortal layers make - from one point a layer in Mortal to five in
Mahayana and Tribulation Transcendence.

**Every Mortal layer is +1 to all five.** On top of the random point, a
cultivator clearing a Mortal layer gains a point in Constitution, Physique,
Agility, Spell Power and Qi Sea alike, and its health and strike follow at
once. Each realm has its own figure on the Wild Cultivators tab (0 above
Mortal, out of the box). Their attributes count as yours do: a point of
Constitution is a point of health, Physique is strike.

**You see them climb.** A notice rises over a cultivator's head when it
climbs - *Layer 5*, or *Breakthrough: Qi Refining Middle*, or in gold
*Breakthrough to Foundation Establishment!* - and fades after three seconds.
With it comes a burst of light that grows with its realm: a few sparks for a
Mortal's layer, three times as many in a column for a new stage, nine times as
many and a ring of fire on the ground for a new realm, and every realm climbed
makes each one bigger and wider. A switch, on by default.

**Throw them a pill and they take it.** Drop a pill at a cultivator's feet
and it picks it up and takes it, one at a time, with the pill's full effect: a
cultivation pill fills its bar (an Immortal one all of it), qi pills its qi,
Rejuvenation heals it, Clear Mind lifts what ails it, Divine Stride and Blood
Burn put their effects on it, a Qi Spring starts its spring, and attribute
pills and free Zhenyuan go into its attributes - health and strike following
at once. A pill it cannot use now, a breakthrough pill say, goes into its pack.
Only pills a player threw; loot on the ground is left alone. A switch, on by
default.

**Spirit beasts climb in layers too.** The cultivation mod's beasts gather qi
on their own and read their stage off it. The Xuan Gui's panel now says its
layer as well - "Qi Refining Early Layer 2" - three to Early, Middle and Late
and one to Peak, read off the same qi, so it always agrees with the stage
shown, and it climbs them as it gathers. Crossing one gives off a burst of
light sized by its realm. A switch, on by default.

**Their Peak opens the way, as yours does.** The cultivation mod lets a
cultivator at the Peak of Qi Refining build its foundation only from the pills
and fruit in its pack - one without them never broke through and stayed at
Peak for good. Now a cultivator that fills its Peak takes the same road you
do: **the foundation tiers the Peak route opens for you** (Human, Blood and
Earth out of the box, a quarter of the bar a tier), or what its pack allows if
that is better - and when its Peak is what opened the tier, it keeps its pills.
It then breaks through with that foundation's perks - its health, its spells,
its strike. The same at the Peak of Foundation Establishment for the golden
core. The tribulation, the lightning and the chance of failing stay the
cultivation mod's own. A switch, on by default.

**A new Mortal starts at layer 1.** The cultivation mod rolls a new
cultivator's sub-stage at random, so a fresh Mortal could appear anywhere on
the ladder. It now starts at Mortal Early, layer 1, with Early's attributes. One
spawned with its sub-stage written into it keeps it; the realms above Mortal
start wherever they are rolled. A switch, on by default.

**They put their cushion down to break through.** A cultivator at the last
layer of a stage with a full bar takes the cultivation cushion out of its pack
and sets it down - 5 to 15 blocks from the edge of a spiritual pond that
reaches out, when one is within 32 blocks, beside itself otherwise - then
breaks through on it
the way the cultivation mod has cultivators do, and picks it up again after.
Wild cultivators used to sit at a full bar for ever, because they never put
their cushion down.

**A tab of their own.** With Config Extension, the *Wild Cultivators* tab holds
all of it, apart from your own pages: layers on or off; which curve their
layers cost - the same as yours, or any of the nine curves for them alone; a
pace that makes them climb faster or slower than you (0.5 is twice as fast,
2.0 half as fast); the cushion switches; and the points a layer pays in every
realm.

**Their own presets.** Config Extension's **Presets** window lists them under
*Wild Cultivators*, after your curves: twice as fast as you, as fast, half as
fast; their layers priced as your own pages say; or any of the nine curves for
them alone. A pace and a curve are separate picks, so you can take one of each.

**They sit by the water to cultivate.** A cultivator with no sect, nothing to
fight and nobody to trade with goes to a spiritual pond that reaches out within
32 blocks, sets its cushion down 5 to 15 blocks from the edge and sits on it.
On the cushion it takes in twice the water's qi (a setting), crosses its
layers and makes its breakthroughs right there. A fight or a trade stands it
up, and it sits back down after. After ten minutes (a setting) it packs the
cushion up and moves on, and rests as long again before it looks for water.

**No cushion left behind.** A cultivator always takes its cushion back up -
after its breakthrough, after its time by the water, and even when it has wandered so
far that the cushion's ground was no longer loaded: it is collected the
moment that ground loads again. One that dies leaves its cushion lying where
the cushion stood, yours for the taking; one that leaves the world any other
way takes it along.

**Nothing meditates on air.** A cultivation cushion whose ground goes - the
leaves under it burned away, the earth dug out - comes down to the first
ground below, and whoever sat on it falls the way anyone falls. Every
cushion, yours too; a wild cultivator still knows its cushion where it
landed and sits back down on it. A setting on the *Their Cushion* page.

**Peace around the cushion.** Nobody starts a fight with a cultivator sitting
on its cushion, or with anyone within 12 blocks of it, and a cultivator
standing in that circle starts none either. Being attacked first lifts it:
whoever is struck answers the one who struck, player or cultivator, for
thirty seconds. A heart demon's trial is never stopped. Every number, and both
switches, are on the Wild Cultivators tab.

## 🪓 Wild cultivators fend for themselves

A wild cultivator with no sect, when it is free - not fighting, trading,
sleeping, sitting on its cushion, about to break through or running with a
treasure - now sees to itself:

**It fells trees.** A living tree near it - the game's own logs standing in
leaves that grew there - comes down whole, log by log from the foot, into its
pack: three seconds a log by hand, two with a wooden axe, one with stone. A
log house is never touched: leaves a player set do not count, and a stand of
more than sixty-four logs is a building, not a tree.

**It crafts.** With enough wood in its pack it sets a crafting table down
beside it, works at it for a moment and takes it back up: logs into planks,
planks into sticks, then **a wooden sword, axe and pickaxe** - four logs make
the lot. With a pickaxe it mines the bare stone it finds at the surface, never
digging under, and makes **stone ones**. The best sword is drawn and the best
armour worn as the cultivation mod itself would have it.

**It works iron.** With a stone pickaxe it mines **iron ore that shows** near
it - in a cliff, a cave mouth, a river bank - three seconds an ore, two with an
iron pickaxe. It builds **a furnace** from eight cobblestone, sets it down
beside it and smelts the ore as a furnace would, ten seconds an ingot, burning
coal first, then planks, then logs, and takes the furnace back up. The ingots
become **an iron sword, pickaxe and axe**, then **a full set of iron armour**,
chest first. Only ore lying in the ground is taken - every block touching it
stone, dirt, gravel, sand, ore, air or water - so ore set into anything built
is left alone.

**It steeps its gear.** Spiritual water within 24 blocks that would raise its
sword or a tool it carries draws it there: it tosses the piece in, waits out
the ten seconds, fishes it back out and wields it. Anyone quick enough can
take it from the water first.

**It takes gear lying about.** A sword it would draw, armour it would wear, a
better axe or pickaxe, or a spiritual vessel lying near it - it goes and
picks it up. Nothing steeping in spiritual water is taken out of it, yours
included.

Nothing is broken where the game's rules keep mobs from changing the world.
Five settings on the Wild Cultivators tab, *Their Craft*: gathering and
crafting, working iron, how far they look (16 blocks), steeping, and taking
gear. Spiritual vessels of their own are still to come (work in progress).

## 🦘 A cultivator jumps higher as they climb

**Mortal's first layer jumps as anyone does. Its ninth jumps five and a half
blocks.** Each layer between is a share of the way, and every realm above
Mortal adds a block and a half more across its layers, up to ten blocks. The
jump is still the game's own, pushed harder: Agility and Jump Boost count on
top, and nothing makes a jump lower. The height your own jump adds is taken off
a fall before it hurts, so jumping on the spot never costs health.

All of it is on a new **Body** page of the Expansion tab: the switch, Mortal's
top, the step a realm, the highest jump and the soft landing.

## 🫁 A cultivator holds their breath longer as they climb

**Mortal's first layer holds it fifteen seconds, as anyone does. Qi Refining
holds it thirty, Foundation Establishment a minute, Golden Core two.** Every
realm doubles it, its layers taking it there a step at a time, up to
twenty-five minutes. A breath at the surface fills the bigger lungs as fast as
anyone's, and the bubbles over the hotbar show the share left: ten for a whole
breath, however long it is.

Three settings in a *Breath* group on the **Body** page: the switch, how many
times longer each realm holds it, and the longest breath - up to about
twenty-seven minutes, the most the game keeps in a save.

## ⚡ Meet your tribulation in the water

Stand in spiritual water when the bolts come and they hit softer: a tenth off
at Inferior, another tenth per grade to six tenths at Supreme, and three
quarters off at Immortal. Never all of it. The bolts also stop throwing you out
of the pool you built to stand in.

## 👁️ Look at the water and it tells you what it is

Point at any spiritual water and two lines appear at the top of the screen: the
grade and the pond size, then how far it reaches and how many blocks of water
are joined. The action bar tells you how many stones are still to go after
every throw.

## 🎚️ The difficulty curves are in the Presets window

With Config Extension installed, its **Presets** button - up beside Hide All
and Show All - lists all nine curves under *Difficulty*, gentlest first, each
with its one line and an **Apply**. Apply fills every values page at once,
exactly as picking the curve from the Curve Preset dropdown does, and the pages
already show the new numbers when the window closes.

The order is by what the whole climb costs, and the dropdown uses it too. Nine
Heavens used to be listed in the middle; across the whole climb it costs more
than Brutal Plus and less than Marathon, so that is where it sits now.

## ⌨️ Two commands for trying things

For operators: **`/realmexpansion steep <grade>`** steeps the stack in your
hand at once, as ten seconds in water of that grade would — the odd Inferior
pill handed back — and says so when the water would do nothing to it.
**`/realmexpansion pond <grade> [radius]`** turns the water around you to that
grade, four blocks out unless you say, sixteen at most: plain water and every
grade of spiritual water, sources staying sources and flowing water keeping
its level. Nothing that is not water is touched, so dig the pond, fill it
with buckets, and give it a grade in one line.
